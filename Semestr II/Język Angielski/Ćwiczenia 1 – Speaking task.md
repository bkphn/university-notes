## Summary
During the pandemic of coronavirus, three Polish scientists: Karol Bacik, Bogdan Bacik, and Grzegorz Sobota, who were trained in **fluid dynamics**, started thinking about optimizing movement in public spaces under the **social distance restrictions**.  

Through mathematical analysis and simulations, they started to study how people **act in crowds**, how they move, dodge, and change direction in **crowded areas**. Researchers also made a several crowd experiments to observe how real pedestrians act.  
 
The research team found a measure called **angular spread**, which determines whether ordered lanes of pedestrians will form or if their movement will remain chaotic. Angular spread refers to the average angle from a pedestrian's starting point to their destination. If the angular spread is less than 13 degrees, pedestrians are likely to form a few clear, **perpendicular lanes**. However, if the angular spread is more than 13 degrees, pedestrians will stay **disordered** and **chaotic**. A fascinating thing about this is that we can see very similar **movement patterns** in the behavior of particles, grains, and fluids.  

The discovery of Polish researchers is expected to have a huge impact on how we plan public spaces, optimizing the **pedestrian flow**.
